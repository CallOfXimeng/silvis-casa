<h3 style="color: #8246af; font-weight: bold; background-color: #d7c6e6; border: 1px solid #aaaaaa; padding: 2px 6px; border-radius: 3px; display: inline-block;">
  1. Modeling timing residual signal
</h3>

This note will calculate the non-Gaussianity arises from the four point function (4PT) of the PTA timing residual. Especially, how to estimate the number of bright sources from the NG part.

The timing residual:

$$
\delta t^p(t)=\sum_{I=1}^{N_f}\sum_{n}^{N_n}\; c^p_{I,n} \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\omega_It+\phi_{I,n})} + \mathrm{c.c.}
\tag{1.1} $$

where, the sub and super scripts:
<div style="border: 2px solid #aaaaaa; background-color: #f5f5f5; padding: 15px; border-radius: 8px; margin: 10px 0;">

- $I$: The $I$-th <span style="color: #00c389; font-weight: bold;">  frequency bin </span>, with $\omega_I=I\cdot 2\pi/T$, $T$ is the total observation time. There are $N_f$ frequency bins in total.
- $p$: The $p$-th pulsar. 
- $n$: The $n$-th  <span style="color: #00c389; font-weight: bold;"> GW source (SMBHB) </span>. There are $N_n$ GW source in total.
</div>

The coefficient $c_{I,n}^p$:

$$
c_{I,n}^p=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{I,n}}{2\mathrm{i}\omega_I}\left[1-\exp(-\mathrm{i}\omega_IL^p(1+\vec{p}^p\cdot \vec{\Omega}_{I,n}))\right]\cdot\left[F^{p,+}(\vec{\Omega}_{I,n})-\mathrm{i}F^{p,\times}(\vec{\Omega}_{I,n})\right].
\tag{1.2} $$

Contains both earth term (one) and pulsar term (exponential). $F$ is the antenna function.

Now, in one given frequency bin, the spectral coefficients:

$$
\mathcal{C}_I^p=\sum_n c_{I,n}^p\exp(\mathrm{i}\phi_{I,n}).
\tag{1.3}$$

So that the timing residual reads:

$$
\delta t^p(t)=\sum_{I}\;\mathcal{C}_{I}^p\exp(\mathrm{i}\omega_It).
\tag{1.4}
$$

Notice that the random phases $\phi_{I,n}$ in (1.3) imply that different GW sources are  <span style="color: #00c389; font-weight: bold;"> incoherent </span>. Besides, we could not directly observe the individual source $c_{I,n}^p$. Only their vector sum is observable.


<h3 style="color: #8246af; font-weight: bold; background-color: #d7c6e6; border: 1px solid #aaaaaa; padding: 2px 6px; border-radius: 3px; display: inline-block;">
  2. Two-point correlation of spectral coefficients
</h3>

From the definition, the correlation (ensemble average of the random phase $\phi$) between two amplitudes:

$$
\left<\mathcal{C}_{I}^{p} (\mathcal{C}_{J}^{q})^*\right>=\delta_{IJ}\delta_{mn}\sum_{n}\;c_{I,n}^p(c_{J,m}^q)^*,\;\text{since } \left<\exp[\mathrm{i}(\phi_{I,n}-\phi_{J,m}]\right>=\delta_{IJ}\delta_{nm}.
\tag{2.5}
$$

Here, we drop the subscript $\phi$ in the ensemble average $\left<\cdots\right>_\phi$ for simplicity. Sum (integrate) over $\Omega$, <span style="color: #00c389; font-weight: bold;"> by ignoring the pulsar term </span>, and define the complex antenna function as $F^{p}(\vec{\Omega})$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left<\mathcal{C}_{I}^{p} (\mathcal{C}_{J}^{q})^*\right>_{\phi,\Omega}&=\frac{1}{4\omega_I^2}\delta_{IJ}\sum_n\; (\mathcal{A}_{I,n})^2\left<F^{p}(\vec\Omega_{I,n})F^q(\vec\Omega_{J,n})\right>_{\Omega}\\
&=\frac{1}{4\omega_I^2}\delta_{IJ}\sum_n\; (\mathcal{A}_{I,n})^2\mu(\gamma^{pq}),
\end{aligned}
\tag{2.6}
$$

where $\gamma^{pq}$ is the angle between pulsar $p$ and $q$, $\mu(\gamma^{pq})$ is the <span style="color: #00c389; font-weight: bold;"> Hellings-Downs curve </span>. Eq. (2.6) could be written in a closed form, as

$$
\left<\mathcal{C}_{I}^{p} (\mathcal{C}_{J}^{q})^*\right>_{\phi,\Omega}=\mathcal{P}_I\Gamma^{pq}, \qquad \mathcal{P}_I\equiv \frac{1}{4\omega_I^2}\sum_n(\mathcal{A}_{I,n})^2.
\tag{2.6*}
$$

<h3 style="color: #8246af; font-weight: bold; background-color: #d7c6e6; border: 1px solid #aaaaaa; padding: 2px 6px; border-radius: 3px; display: inline-block;">
  3. Four-point correlation of spectral coefficients
</h3>

Now, the non-Gaussianity could be characterized by the four-point correlation. Especially, the connecting parts. Follow the similar method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
    \left<\mathcal{C}_{I}^{p} (\mathcal{C}_{J}^{q})^*\mathcal{C}_{K}^{r} (\mathcal{C}_{L}^{s})^*\right>=\sum_{nmtu}\;&c_{I,n}^p(c_{J,m}^q)^*c_{K,t}^r(c_{L,u}^s)^*\\
     &\cdot\left<\exp[\mathrm{i}(\phi_{I,n}-\phi_{J,m}+\phi_{k,t}-\phi_{L,u})]\right>.
\end{aligned}
\tag{3.7}
$$

This requires that

$$ 
\phi_{I,n}-\phi_{J,m}+\phi_{K,t}-\phi_{L,u}=0.
\tag{3.8}
$$

Since the phases are random, we must have $\phi_{I,n}+\phi_{K,t}=\phi_{J,m}+\phi_{L,u}$, which could be divided into two cases:
<div style="border: 2px solid #aaaaaa; background-color: #f5f5f5; padding: 15px; border-radius: 8px; margin: 10px 0;">

- **Case A**: Two pairs equal: $I = J, K = L$  or $I = L, K = J$, but $I\ne K$.
- **Case B** Four terms equal: $I=J=K=L$. 
</div>

For **case A**, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
    \left<\text{4-points, A}\right> =& \delta_{IJ}\delta_{KL}(1-\delta_{IK})\sum_{nt}\; c_{I,n}^p(c_{I,n}^q)^*c_{K,t}^r(c_{K,t}^s)^*\\
    &\qquad +\delta_{IL}\delta_{JK}(1-\delta_{IK})\sum_{nt}\; c_{I,n}^p(c_{I,n}^s)^*c_{K,t}^r(c_{K,t}^q)^*\\
    =&\left<\mathcal{C}_{I}^p(\mathcal{C}_{J}^q)^*\right>\left<\mathcal{C}_{K}^r(\mathcal{C}_{L}^s)^*\right>+\left<\mathcal{C}_{I}^p(\mathcal{C}_{L}^s)^*\right>\left<\mathcal{C}_{K}^r(\mathcal{C}_{J}^q)^*\right>.
\end{aligned} 
\tag{3.9}
$$ 

The second equality is the Gaussian part expanded by Wick theorem, whose $\Omega$ average is given by

$$
\left<\text{4PT}_{A}\right>_{\phi, \Omega}=\mathcal{P}_I\mathcal{P}_K(\Gamma^{pq}\Gamma^{rs}+\Gamma^{ps}\Gamma^{rq}).
\tag{3.10}
$$

For **case B**, it is a bit more difficult. Notice that the four amplitude is in the <span style="color: #00c389; font-weight: bold;">  same frequency bin </span>, i.e., $I=J=K=L$. We also have two sub-cases, which are non-Gaussian
<div style="border: 2px solid #aaaaaa; background-color: #f5f5f5; padding: 15px; border-radius: 8px; margin: 10px 0;">

- **Case B1**: Gaussian case $i = j, k = l$  or $i = l, k = j$, but $i\ne k$.
- **Case B2** The contribution from **one bright source**, i.e., $i=j=k=l$ 
</div>

Following the similar way, **case B1** reduces to

$$
\left<\text{4PT}_{B1}\right>_{\phi, \Omega}=\mathcal{K}_I(\Gamma^{pq}\Gamma^{rs}+\Gamma^{ps}\Gamma^{rq}),\qquad \mathcal{K}_I\equiv \sum_n\frac{|\mathcal{A}_{I,n}|^4}{16\omega_I^4}.
\tag{3.11}
$$

And for **case B2**, this is a <span style="color: #00c389; font-weight: bold;">  non-Gaussian connecting term </span>:

$$
\left<\text{4PT}_{B2}\right>_{\phi, \Omega}=\mathcal{K}_I\cdot\left<F^{p}(\vec\Omega_{I,n})F^q(\vec\Omega_{I,n})F^{p}(\vec\Omega_{I,n})F^q(\vec\Omega_{I,n})\right>_{\Omega}
\tag{3.12}
$$

This is calculated in [2603.12311](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.12311).


<h3 style="color: #8246af; font-weight: bold; background-color: #d7c6e6; border: 1px solid #aaaaaa; padding: 2px 6px; border-radius: 3px; display: inline-block;">
  4. From non-Gaussianity to the source number
</h3>

Define the dimensionless, normalized NG parameter:

$$
g_N=\frac{\left<\text{4PT}_{B1}\right>_{\phi, \Omega}+\left<\text{4PT}_{B2}\right>_{\phi, \Omega}}{\left<\text{4PT}_{A}\right>_{\phi, \Omega}}.
\tag{4.13}
$$

The full expression of $g_N$ could be really complicated. Here, we consider two limits:
- **Limit 1**: No bright sources, all are faint;
- **Limit 2**: $\mathcal{A}_n$ dominated by a few bright sources.

Re-arrange $g_N$ as

$$
g_N = \frac{\mathcal{K}_I}{\mathcal{P}_I\mathcal{P}_K}\cdot \left(1+\frac{\left<F^4\right>_{\phi, \Omega}}{\Gamma^{pq}\Gamma^{rs}+\Gamma^{ps}\Gamma^{rq}}\right)\equiv  \frac{\mathcal{K}_I}{\mathcal{P}_I\mathcal{P}_K}[1+\mathcal{F}^{pqrs}(\gamma)].
\tag{4.14}
$$

Now, first, under the **limit 1**. We have

$$
\mathcal{P}_I\equiv \frac{1}{4\omega_I^2}\sum_n(\mathcal{A}_{I,n})^2=\frac{N_n}{4\omega_I^2}\left<\mathcal{A}_I^2\right>_{\mathrm{so}}, \qquad \text{so = GW source}.
\tag{4.15}
$$

And

$$
\mathcal{K}_I\equiv \frac{1}{16\omega_I^4}\sum_n(\mathcal{A}_{I,n})^4=\frac{N_n}{16\omega_I^4}\left<\mathcal{A}_I^4\right>_{\mathrm{so}}.
\tag{4.15}
$$

Substituting into (1.14), we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
  g_{N}&=\frac{1}{N_k}\frac{\left<\mathcal{A}_I^4\right>_{\mathrm{so}}}{\left<\mathcal{A}_I^2\right>_{\mathrm{so}}\left<\mathcal{A}_K^2\right>_{\mathrm{so}}}[1+\mathcal{F}^{pqrs}(\gamma)]\\[4mm]
  &=[1+\mathcal{F}^{pqrs}(\gamma)]\cdot \begin{cases}
    \dfrac{1}{N_k}, \qquad &\text{Same amplitude;}\\[3mm]
    \dfrac{3}{N_k}\dfrac{\left<\mathcal{A}_I^2\right>_{\mathrm{so}}}{\left<\mathcal{A}_K^2\right>_{\mathrm{so}}}, \quad &\text{ Gaussian distribution.}
  \end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.16}
$$

The effective source number could be defined as the inverse of the normalized non-Gaussianity, i.e.

$$
N_{\rm eff}\equiv g_N^{-1}.
\tag{4.17}
$$

On the other hand, we are more interested in the **limit 2**, where we assume that the source are dominated by bright source $N_{I}^b$, whose amplitudes are $\mathcal{A}_{I,n}^b$. Along with faint background $N_{I, f}$ with $\mathcal{A}_{I,n}^f$. The two qualities:

$$
\begin{aligned}
  \mathcal{P}_I&=\frac{1}{4\omega_I^2} \sum_{n=1}^{N_n^b}\;(\mathcal{A}_{I,n}^b)^2+\frac{1}{4\omega_I^2} \sum_{n=1}^{N_n^f}\;(\mathcal{A}_{I,n}^f)^2\\
  &\qquad=\frac{1}{4\omega_I^2} \left\{ \sum_{n=1}^{N_n^b}\;(\mathcal{A}_{I,n}^b)^2+N_n^f\left<(\mathcal{A}_{I,n}^f)^2\right>_{\rm so}\right\};\\
  \mathcal{K}_I&=\frac{1}{16\omega_I^4} \sum_{n=1}^{N_n^b}\;(\mathcal{A}_{I,n}^b)^4+\frac{1}{16\omega_I^4} \sum_{n=1}^{N_n^f}\;(\mathcal{A}_{I,n}^f)^4\\
  &\qquad =\frac{1}{16\omega_I^4}\left\{ \sum_{n=1}^{N_n^b}\;(\mathcal{A}_{I,n}^b)^4+N_n^f\left<(\mathcal{A}_{I,n}^f)^4\right>_{\rm so}\right\}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.18}
$$

The ratio in (4.16) is given by

$$
\frac{\mathcal{K}_I}{\mathcal{P}_I\mathcal{P}_K}=\frac{ \sum_{n=1}^{N_n^b}\;(\mathcal{A}_{I,n}^b)^4+N_n^f\left<(\mathcal{A}_{I,n}^f)^4\right>_{\rm so}}{\left\{ \sum_{n=1}^{N_n^b}\;(\mathcal{A}_{I,n}^b)^2+N_n^f\left<(\mathcal{A}_{I,n}^f)^2\right>_{\rm so}\right\}\left\{ \sum_{t=1}^{N_t^b}\;(\mathcal{A}_{K,t}^b)^2+N_t^f\left<(\mathcal{A}_{K,t}^f)^2\right>_{\rm so}\right\}}\cdot\frac{\omega_K^2}{\omega_I^2}\\
\tag{4.19}
$$

Series expand the ratio at small $\left<(\mathcal{A}^f)^2\right>_{\rm so}$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
  \frac{\mathcal{K}_I}{\mathcal{P}_I\mathcal{P}_K}\simeq&\frac{\sum_{n=1}^{N_n^b}\;(\mathcal{A}_{I,n}^b)^4}{\sum_{n=1}^{N_n^b}\;(\mathcal{A}_{I,n}^b)^2\cdot \sum_{t=1}^{N_t^b}\;(\mathcal{A}_{K,t}^b)^2}\\
  &\qquad \cdot\left(1-\frac{N_n^f\left<(\mathcal{A}_{I,n}^f)^2\right>_{\rm so}}{\sum_{n=1}^{N_n^b}\;(\mathcal{A}_{I,n}^b)^2}-\frac{N_t^f\left<(\mathcal{A}_{K,t}^f)^2\right>_{\rm so}}{\sum_{t=1}^{N_t^b}\;(\mathcal{A}_{K,t}^b)^2}\right)\cdot \frac{\omega_K^2}{\omega_I^2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.20}
$$

The same $1/g_N$ should also give $N_{\rm eff}$. However, this value is less accurate than the faint background case with out modeling the bright sources.
